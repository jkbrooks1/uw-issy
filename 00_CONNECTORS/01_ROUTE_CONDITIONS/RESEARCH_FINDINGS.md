## Route and jurisdiction summary

The canonical GPX (`data/route/UnivWA-Issaquah.gpx`) traces a ~33.5-mile corridor from the University of Washington through the Burke-Gilman Trail, Sammamish River Trail, Marymoor Park, and East Lake Sammamish Trail to Issaquah. Using the verified GPX-derived path shape supplied in the work order, cross-referenced against agency jurisdiction descriptions found in research, the following section/jurisdiction model was confirmed and refined:

This confirms the work order's directionally-consistent 9/10-segment model is correct in shape and refines it with agency ownership: **King County Parks owns the trail corridor itself (Burke-Gilman north of Seattle, all of Sammamish River Trail, all of Marymoor Park, all of East Lake Sammamish Trail) as part of its "Leafline Trails Network."** The cities the trail passes through (Bothell, Woodinville, Redmond, Sammamish, Kenmore, Lake Forest Park) are adjacent-jurisdiction landowners/permit authorities for construction affecting the corridor but generally do not independently operate the trail's alert channel — with the notable exception of the City of Sammamish, which actively co-publishes construction content that intersects the trail (see SAM-01).

Within Seattle city limits, the Burke-Gilman Trail is managed jointly by SDOT and Seattle Parks and Recreation. The segment through the UW campus is managed by the University itself.

## Discovery method

1. Started from the work order's list of candidate agencies (Seattle DOT, Seattle Parks, UW, King County Parks, King County Road Services, King County regional trails/Leafline, six named cities, Marymoor Park, WSDOT, Sound Transit).
2. Used WebSearch to locate each agency's trail-specific page, open-data presence, and any historical closure coverage (to confirm which agencies actually publish route-relevant content, versus which only appear in general municipal directories).
3. Used WebFetch to directly retrieve and inspect a subset of the most promising candidate URLs, recording actual page content, structure, and (for API endpoints) actual HTTP/service response behavior.
4. Cross-checked findings across independent sources where possible (e.g. the active 2026 East Lake Sammamish Trail closure was independently confirmed via King County's own page, the City of Sammamish's 2026 construction-projects page, and — as a non-authoritative corroboration only — Seattle Bike Blog).
5. Explicitly declined to mark a source VERIFIED unless it was directly fetched in this cycle; sources known only via search-result snippets are marked PARTIALLY_VERIFIED or UNVERIFIED, per the standing research-quality requirement.

## Verified source landscape (high level)

- **King County Parks (Leafline Trails Network) is the single most important agency** for this route: it owns and manages the Burke-Gilman Trail outside Seattle, the entire Sammamish River Trail, Marymoor Park, and the entire East Lake Sammamish Trail. Its primary current channel for closure/construction alerts is a set of trail-specific `kingcounty.gov` pages (KC-01, KC-02, KC-03), not an API and not the older `kingcountyparks.org` WordPress blog (KC-04), which was found to be stale (no posts after 2021) and is rejected as an operational source.
- **No agency in this corridor publishes a verified, machine-readable, real-time closure/status API.** The SDOT ArcGIS Street Use service (SEA-01) exists but returned a service-not-started error on direct test. The Seattle Socrata SDOT GIS Datasets catalog entry (SEA-02) and King County's GIS Open Data trail_line layer (KC-05) could not be confirmed to expose closure/status attributes (as opposed to static geometry) in this research cycle, and their landing pages are JavaScript-rendered, blocking a plain-fetch confirmation.
- **City of Issaquah's Civic Alerts / Traffic Alerts category (ISS-01) is the one genuinely DOCUMENTED_FEED-class municipal source confirmed in this cycle**, with a working RSS option and an email/SMS "Notify Me" subscription, covering the Issaquah approach/terminus segment. It is general traffic alerts, not trail-specific, so it requires street-name keyword filtering.
- **City of Sammamish's annual construction-projects page (SAM-01) is a verified, current, prose-format source** that directly corroborates and supplements the King County East Lake Sammamish Trail closure notice.
- **UW Facilities and UW Transportation each maintain general blogs/alert pages** that occasionally reference Burke-Gilman Trail or campus-adjacent street closures, but neither is trail-specific; both require keyword filtering and neither alone is MVP-grade.
- **WSDOT and Sound Transit are narrow, situational sources.** WSDOT is only relevant at discrete state-highway grade crossings (not yet enumerated against the GPX in this cycle) and requires a registered developer Access Code that was not obtained. Sound Transit's East Link construction near Marymoor/Redmond, historically a major disruption risk, has now largely concluded (2 Line stations opened May 2025), reducing its current relevance.
- **A confirmed and significant coverage gap exists for the City of Bothell, City of Kenmore, City of Lake Forest Park, and City of Woodinville** — all four physically host route segments, but no dedicated, verified alerts channel was found for any of them in this research cycle (see SOURCE_GAPS.md). Historical news-media coverage (not agency-published) was the only material found referencing past trail work in these jurisdictions.
- **Seattle Bike Blog (independent, non-governmental) is a useful discovery/lead signal** that reliably surfaces trail closures early, but per the standing project rule that community/manual-review sources must not auto-publish route status, it is rejected as an acquisition source and documented only as a cross-check tool for human reviewers.
- **A promising but unresolved lead exists in King County's GovDelivery (Granicus) bulletin system** (OTH-02): per-topic RSS feeds are confirmed to exist in this platform generally, but the specific topic ID for King County Parks trail alerts was not identified in this cycle.

## Follow-up verification cycle findings (2026-07-28)

This section records what changed in the follow-up verification cycle, per the work order's four specific tasks. Full detail is in API_AND_FEED_TEST_RESULTS.md (Tests 11-20), SOURCE_REGISTRY.md/.json, and SOURCE_GAPS.md; this is a summary for research-narrative continuity.

1. **The four small cities (Bothell, Kenmore, Lake Forest Park, Woodinville) were each directly investigated.** None operates a dedicated trail-closure/construction-alert channel (RSS, GovDelivery topic, or GIS portal) distinct from a general municipal notification system. The one meaningful exception is the City of Kenmore, which hosts a real, WSDOT-funded "Burke Gilman/SR522 Accessibility Project" page directly describing Burke-Gilman Trail infrastructure (an existing pedestrian underpass at 73rd Ave NE and a signed SR-522 at-grade crossing used in official detours) — a genuinely useful SECONDARY source (OTH-03B), not merely a general city notification list. A significant correction was also made: the City of Woodinville's actual domain is `woodinville.gov`, not `woodinvillewa.gov` as recorded in the prior cycle (the latter does not resolve at all).

2. **WSDOT is confirmed relevant to this corridor**, resolving the prior cycle's open question. Two specific, named crossing zones were identified by cross-referencing the supplied GPX turn-cue waypoints against real WSDOT/city project documentation: (a) SR-522 (Bothell Way) running alongside the Burke-Gilman Trail through Kenmore, and (b) the I-405/SR-522 interchange between Bothell and Woodinville, where the Sammamish River Trail passes through active construction requiring flagger-controlled crossings. WSDOT-01 remains SECONDARY (not MVP) because its live API still requires an unobtained developer Access Code, and because KC-01/KC-02 already cover these segments at the trail-status level — but its relevance is no longer speculative.

3. **SDOT's two candidate GIS/open-data sources (SEA-01, SEA-02) were retested and are now definitively resolved, not merely re-flagged as still-blocked.** SEA-01 (ArcGIS MapServer) failed identically across both known endpoint variants — a confirmed persistent failure, reclassified to REJECT. SEA-02 (Socrata "SDOT GIS Datasets") is now confirmed to be a catalog/directory page describing 60+ other datasets, with no tabular data of its own — also reclassified to REJECT as a direct source (though it remains a legitimate lead for a future cycle to identify a genuinely relevant downstream dataset).

4. **King County's GovDelivery (Granicus) bulletin system yielded real, positive evidence this cycle** — two directly-fetched bulletins ("Regional Trail Alert: Sammamish River Trail Closure in Woodinville" and an update on Sammamish River Trail construction work in Bothell) confirm the channel is real, active, and specifically on-topic for this route. The specific subscribable topic ID for automation remains unresolved: the GovDelivery subscriber page only reveals its topic list after a real email address is submitted into the interactive signup form, which a passive research fetch cannot get past. OTH-02 is reclassified from BLOCKED/UNRESOLVED to PARTIALLY_VERIFIED/SECONDARY.

5. **The Issaquah-approach street list was derived from the supplied GPX turn-cue waypoints, but this cycle's corrected canonical GPX (see item 8 below) revealed the original list mislabeled one street.** "Northeast 65th Street" was included as "Issaquah-approach" but is actually a Redmond/Bear Creek-area street ~15 miles north of Issaquah — an error independent of the GPX itself, since even the original v1 GPX placed that waypoint near Redmond, not Issaquah. The corrected street list (East Lake Sammamish Parkway Northeast, East Lake Sammamish Lane Northeast, East Lake Sammamish Trail) is now in ROUTE_SECTION_SOURCE_MAPPING.md.

6. **The MVP source set is unchanged (KC-01, KC-02, KC-03, SAM-01, ISS-01, 5 sources).** No newly-verified source this cycle offered a better trail-status signal than the existing King County Leafline pages for any segment; the follow-up work resolved ambiguity and added confirmed SECONDARY context rather than expanding MVP coverage.

7. **Third follow-up cycle (2026-07-28): all 5 MVP sources are now directly VERIFIED, closing the last remaining MVP-grade verification gap.** KC-02 was directly re-fetched for the first time (previously known only via a search-result URL, PARTIALLY_VERIFIED by analogy to KC-01/KC-03) and confirmed live, current, and structurally identical to its sibling pages, with no active alert banner at fetch time. UW-02 was also directly re-fetched and upgraded to VERIFIED, but with an honest correction: its four currently-listed alerts (Aug 2025 through Dec 2024) contain no mention of the Burke-Gilman Trail, contradicting the prior cycle's search-snippet-based claim of trail-relevant content. The exact SEA-03 "Burke-Gilman Trail Repairs" subpage URL was confirmed via direct fetch of the parent page's link markup, though its own body content could not be retrieved via WebFetch in two attempts this cycle. DOM/extraction structure was directly observed on all 5 MVP sources: none use a semantic alert component or machine-parseable date microformat, and only ISS-01 offers a stable numeric per-item URL (`/m/newsflash/Home/Detail/{id}`) — KC-01/02/03 and SAM-01 have no persistent per-alert identifier and would require whole-page diffing in any future scraper. The GovDelivery topic-ID question (OTH-02) was probed via four additional independent angles (more bulletin discovery, a numbered signup URL, bulletin-page topic metadata, and GovDelivery's own developer API documentation) and remains genuinely unresolved — confirmed this cycle to be a structural GovDelivery platform limitation (topic listing requires authenticated API access or a completed subscribe flow with a real email), not a research-effort shortfall.

8. **Fourth follow-up cycle (2026-07-28): the project owner supplied a corrected canonical GPX** (`v2.UnivWA-Issaquah.gpx`), now installed as `data/route/UnivWA-Issaquah.gpx` (prior version archived, not deleted, at `data/route/archive/UnivWA-Issaquah.v1.20260728.gpx`). The correction replaces an inaccurate mid-route jog through "Bear Creek Trail" and "Northeast 65th Street" near Redmond with a direct "Marymoor Connector Trail" path that actually routes through Marymoor Park before rejoining the East Lake Sammamish Trail — matching the route's stated description ("...Sammamish River Trail → Marymoor Park → East Lake Sammamish Trail...") more faithfully than the original GPX did. Total distance increased from 33.49 mi to 33.83 mi; the overall bounding box is unchanged (lat 47.55207-47.75889, lon -122.3057 to -122.04414), so no prior geographic/jurisdiction/bbox-based finding in this document is invalidated by the correction, but the Issaquah-approach street list (item 5 above) required a direct fix, and "Marymoor Connector Trail" should be added as a named-trail-segment-matching term for the Marymoor Park section in any future scraper. Trail ownership of "Marymoor Connector Trail" is presumed to be King County Parks (consistent with the Sammamish River Trail and East Lake Sammamish Trail it connects) but has not been independently confirmed and should not be assumed without verification in a future cycle.

9. **Fifth follow-up cycle (2026-07-28): the MVP source set grew from 5 to 7 for the first time**, following direct ArcGIS REST verification of a second user-supplied candidate-source list. Two new geometry-capable DIRECT_API sources reached MVP grade: **REDM-01** (City of Redmond `Traffic/Alerts` FeatureServer — real, live, 3 active alerts confirmed, fills a real gap since Redmond owns 3 route segments and had no dedicated source before) and **ISS-03** (City of Issaquah `PWProjectsCurrentYearConstructionPublic` — a live geometry query against the route's own bounding box returned a genuinely on-route project, "East Lake Sammamish Pkwy Drainage Improvement Project"). Two more real sources were added as SECONDARY (ISS-04, a broader Issaquah development-permit database; SAM-02/SAM-03, Sammamish's building-permit and capital-improvement-project databases). This cycle also directly refuted two claims from the candidate list rather than accepting them uncritically: Seattle's claimed working `SDOT_Bikes/MapServer` returned the identical "Service not started" error as our already-rejected `SDOT_StreetUse_V2` (confirming the entire Seattle ArcGIS Server instance is down, not just one service), and Redmond's claimed `DataSets/Landbase/MapServer` "Trail" layer returned HTTP 404 (does not exist). A methodology error was also caught and corrected mid-cycle: an initial unscoped query against Issaquah's ArcGIS Hub search API returned irrelevant results from unrelated organizations (a Maryland town, a Canadian city) before being properly scoped to Issaquah's own ArcGIS org ID.

10. **Sixth follow-up cycle (2026-07-29): OTH-02 (GovDelivery) formally CLOSED, not resolved positively.** The project owner confirmed GovDelivery is not accessible to them, which forecloses both remaining paths documented in prior cycles (a human subscribe flow with a monitored email, or a direct King County Parks communications inquiry). OTH-02 is reclassified from SECONDARY/PARTIALLY_VERIFIED to REJECT/BLOCKED. This is disclosed as a closed gap rather than continuing to carry it as an open action item indefinitely — the real, on-topic bulletin content confirmed in the third cycle remains true for the historical record, but this source will not be pursued further, since KC-01/02/03 already provide direct official coverage of the same trail segments.

## Overlap with other lanes

- Lane 06 (Trail Infrastructure Status) will cover the same physical bridges/culverts/boardwalks as longer-term capital-asset condition tracking; Lane 01's concern with the same structures is limited to whether current construction/repair work is actively blocking passage right now (e.g. the George Davis Creek culvert replacement project is a Lane 06 capital-asset item but also a Lane 01 active-closure item during its construction window).
- Lane 05 (Flood Conditions) and Lane 04 (Wildfire) may occasionally cause route closures (e.g. flood-related trail closures along the Sammamish River), but per the CDM source philosophy carried over structurally (not substantively) from the reference project, an official flood or fire warning does not automatically imply route closure — Lane 01 should only register CONFIRMED_ROUTE_IMPACT when an official source explicitly states a trail/route closure, not merely a general hazard advisory.
- Lane 07 (Government/Safety Alerts) may carry emergency-related road closures (e.g. police activity, collision response) that also affect route passability; Lane 01 should treat such items as CONFIRMED_ROUTE_IMPACT only when they name a route segment directly, and otherwise defer to Lane 07 for general public-safety framing.
